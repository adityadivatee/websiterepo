Real-Time 3D & Virtual Production with Unreal Engine 5

By Aditya Divte October 2026 Virtual Production & VFX

For decades, the visual effects (VFX) industry operated on a rigid post-production paradigm: live-action footage was captured against monochrome green screens, followed by weeks or months of rotoscoping, camera tracking, and offline multi-pass rendering. Today, the convergence of real-time graphics engines and high-density LED volumes has fundamentally reorganized cinematic production.

At Aditya Divte Production, our research and creative pipeline leverages Unreal Engine 5 (UE5) to bridge the gap between physical stage photography and photorealistic virtual environments.

"Virtual production does not replace the camera; it liberates it, bringing the post-production rendering phase directly into the viewfinder during live principal photography."

The Core Pillars of Unreal Engine 5 in Production

The transition from offline raytracing (such as Arnold, V-Ray, or RenderMan) to real-time simulation was historically constrained by geometric limitations and pre-baked lighting. Unreal Engine 5 dismantled these bottlenecks through two foundational architectural innovations:

1. Nanite: Continuous Level of Detail (LOD) Streaming

Prior to Nanite, digital environment artists spent up to 40% of their production schedules manually authoring polygon LODs, baking normal maps, and optimizing high-poly ZBrush sculpts. Nanite renders millions to billions of micro-polygons in real time by employing a hierarchical cluster tree rendered via a compute rasterizer.

In our production workflows, this allows us to ingest raw photogrammetry captures of ancient ruins, rugged terrain, and architectural facades without decimation. Film directors can dolly the physical camera from a wide landscape shot directly into a millimeter-scale macro shot of sandstone texture without encountering geometric pop-in or memory crashes.

2. Lumen: Real-Time Dynamic Global Illumination

Light is the definitive ingredient of cinematic realism. Traditional game engines relied on static lightmaps baked overnight into UV coordinates. In contrast, Lumen calculates infinite diffuse bounces and specular reflections dynamically using software ray tracing and hardware-accelerated BVH structures.

When a director requests a time-of-day shift—transitioning from golden hour to dusk—the virtual sun's trajectory updates instantaneously. Bounce lighting off canyon walls, atmospheric scattering, and colored spill naturally adjust on the physical actors, completely eliminating the synthetic disconnect common in traditional green-screen composites.

In-Camera Visual Effects (ICVFX) Workflows

The pinnacle of real-time virtual production is the In-Camera Visual Effects (ICVFX) volume. Here is how our studio structures the technical pipeline:

  • Genlock Synchronized LED Volumes: High-refresh LED panels surround the physical soundstage, displaying the virtual Unreal Engine environment behind the physical set dressing.
  • Sub-Millimeter Optical Camera Tracking: Infrared optical tracking sensors (such as Vicon or OptiTrack) track the physical cinema camera’s 6-DoF spatial position, lens focal length, and aperture.
  • Real-Time Inner Frustum Perspective: The Unreal Engine camera renders an "inner frustum"—a precise, perspective-corrected slice of the 3D world aligned exactly to the camera's sensor line of sight. As the camera pans, the perspective on the LED wall shifts with real-world parallax.

Cost, Speed, and Creative Autonomy

While high-end virtual production soundstages require significant upfront engineering, the downstream efficiencies are profound:

  1. Zero Post-Production Green Spill: Actors are lit by genuine light emitted from the virtual environment on the LED panels, resolving skin-tone keying issues.
  2. Actor Immersiveness: Performers act within visible, responsive environments rather than staring at blank green cloth, elevating emotional authenticity.
  3. Immediate Editorial Review: Cinematographers and directors walk away from the day’s shoot with final or near-final composite shots ready for the editorial suite.

Looking Forward: AI & Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs)

The next frontier for Aditya Divte Production involves integrating Gaussian Splatting and Neural Radiance Fields directly into real-time Unreal Engine volumes. By synthesizing complex real-world volumetric lighting and transparent foliage at 60+ FPS, our studio continues to push the boundaries of visual storytelling in India and beyond.
